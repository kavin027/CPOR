\paragraph{Where the extra freedom changes the release.}
Let $l$ and $h$ denote the componentwise minimum and maximum of the archived reference and candidate witnesses at the current issue. The observed forecasts attainable by componentwise interpolation lie in $\{Sw:l\le w\le h\}$ before imposing the release intervals. We compared the stored Full prediction $p$ with this set using observed-coordinate tolerances $\delta_i=2(2\times10^{-6}+10^{-8}|p_i|)$. A coordinate below $(Sl)_i-\delta_i$ or above $(Sh)_i+\delta_i$ suffices to exclude the box. Otherwise a bounded feasibility program searches for any witness. We accept an explicit witness within tolerance or verify a separating linear functional against the entire box. All 18,144 issues were classified. The test keeps both endpoint witnesses fixed and allows alternative representations of the published forecast within that box.

In the continuous ECommerce streams the stored Full witness left the latent box at 1,287 of 1,296 issues. At 1,230 of these issues a different witness within the box reproduced the observed forecast within tolerance; only 57 releases required movement outside the box. In RH, 989 releases were outside, and 938 of them required a joint separation test. On Wiki2, 1,287 continuous releases left the box. Of the 53,046 node--issue pairs where Full crossed the scalar range $[(Sl)_i,(Sh)_i]$ beyond tolerance, Full's actual absolute error was smaller than Independent's in 24,244 and larger in 28,802. At the bottom level Full improved 69 of 150 Wiki2 series when each series' RMSE was averaged across the nine streams. The largest bottom-series deterioration was 5.212\%. The corresponding numbers of improving bottom series were 22/32 on Labour, 12/30 on M5 and 166/200 on Traffic. On the AEMO continuous streams, all 1,294 individual range crossings were at the five regions, and Full was more accurate on 847 of them. For Tasmania it was more accurate on only 27 of 85 crossings.

\paragraph{Current action and earlier releases.}
To hold the available allowance fixed, we replayed Independent once at the start of every issue from Full's own realized allowance. We scored those actions against the next observations and settled the original Full release. Let $M_I$ and $M_F$ be the online path scores for Independent and Full, and $M_*$ the score formed from these matched-state actions. We split $100(M_I-M_F)/M_I$ into $100(M_*-M_F)/M_I$ and $100(M_I-M_*)/M_I$. In continuous evaluation the two terms contributed 0.1140 and 0.0515 percentage points; in windows they contributed 0.1134 and 0.0203. These terms use Full's path as the reference for this diagnostic; a policy that actually released the matched-state actions would develop a different allowance.

For the same-state target-distance check we use $D(p)=\sum_i(p_i-z_i)^2/v_i$, where $v_i$ is the archived projection variance. The stored Full release had $(D_F-D_I)/(1+D_I)>10^{-6}$ in 31 of 18,144 same-state comparisons. The largest normalized excess was $2.073\times10^{-6}$ and the largest absolute excess was $7.853\times10^{-6}$ in weighted squared-distance units. These small reversals concern the numerically accepted releases and their interior margins. The ideal projection comparison applies to exact minimizers on nested sets.
